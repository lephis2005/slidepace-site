How to add a timer to Google Slides

Last updated: 18 October 2026

Google Slides™ has no built-in countdown that your audience can see. The presenter view does show a small elapsed-time clock, but only on your own screen. To show a timer on the slides, there are four common methods. Here is how each one works, with its limits.

1. Insert a YouTube countdown video

  1. Open the slide, then Insert › Video.
  2. Search YouTube for “5 minute timer” (or the duration you need) and insert the video.
  3. In Format options › Video playback, choose to play it automatically when the slide is presented.

Advantages

  • Free, nothing to install.
  • The audience sees it.

Limits

  • One fixed duration per video, and only on that slide: it stops when you change slides.
  • Needs an internet connection; some videos show the YouTube interface or ads.
  • Pausing or adding a minute means clicking the video during your talk.

2. Insert an animated GIF timer

  1. Find or create a countdown GIF of the right length.
  2. Insert › Image and place it on the slide.

Advantages

  • Simple and silent.
  • The audience sees it.

Limits

  • A GIF loops: it restarts at the end, and you cannot pause or reset it.
  • It starts when the slide is shown, and only exists on that slide.
  • Long timers make heavy files.

3. Use the SlidePace extension

  1. Add SlidePace to Chrome (free).
  2. In the extension popup, choose the duration, the mode (countdown or stopwatch), the warning time, the size and the corner.
  3. Start your slideshow: the timer appears on top of the slides.

Advantages

  • Works on any presentation, on every slide, without editing your slides.
  • Start, pause, reset or add/remove a minute with on-screen buttons or keyboard shortcuts (Alt+Shift+S to start or pause).
  • Turns orange before the end, red at zero, then counts the overtime.
  • No account; the free version of the extension collects no data and sends nothing anywhere (with SlidePace Pro, only the license ID is checked, about once a month).

Limits

  • Needs the extension on the computer that runs the slideshow (Google Chrome, or another browser that installs extensions from the Chrome Web Store).
  • The timer is part of the slideshow screen: remote viewers only see it if you share that screen.

SlidePace is our extension: we have tried to describe its limits as plainly as the others’.

4. Other Chrome extensions

  1. Search the Chrome Web Store for “Google Slides timer”.
  2. Some extensions work differently: for example, Slides Timer turns text you type in a slide (such as <<5:00->>) into a timer.

Advantages

  • Some offer in-slide timers, clocks or other effects.

Limits

  • Timers written into the slides must be added to each deck and slide where you need them.
  • Check each extension’s permissions and the privacy section of its store listing before installing.

For a one-off five-minute break on a single slide, a video or a GIF is enough. If you present often, need the timer on every slide, or want to pause and adjust it while you speak, an extension is more convenient.
